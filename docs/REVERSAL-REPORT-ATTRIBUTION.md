# Reversal and Cancellation Report Attribution Specification

Documenting the design decisions and query attribution models for sale cancellations and payment reversals in Aira (Ticket #13), establishing conventions for D4 reporting queries (Ticket #15).

---

## 1. Core Principles

1. **Strict Offline Ledger Integrity**: Record cancellations and reversals are accounting ledger operations. They correct on-device data and restore inventory; they **never trigger real external refunds** (no banking/GCash APIs or mock transfers).
2. **Stock Restored Exactly Once**: Cancelling a sale increases on-hand inventory by each line item's sold quantity and records an `inventory_movements` row with `movement_type = 'sale_cancellation'`. A sale cannot be cancelled more than once.
3. **Audit History Preservation**: Records are never hard-deleted (`DELETE`). Cancelled sales are marked `status = 'cancelled'` with `cancelled_at` and `cancellation_reason`. Reversed repayments are marked `status = 'reversed'` with `reversed_at` and `reversal_reason`.
4. **Paid-Down Credit Protection**: A credit sale that has already received repayment allocations cannot be cancelled without first reviewing and reversing those allocations, preventing orphan payments and distorted customer balances.

---

## 2. Report Attribution Models

When a transaction occurring on Date $T_1$ (e.g., Monday) is cancelled or reversed on Date $T_2$ (e.g., Friday), reporting queries need clear attribution rules. Aira defines two complementary reporting views:

### A. Cashflow & Activity Ledger (Event-Date Attribution)
*Focus: What physical cash and inventory movements happened during the selected calendar period?*

- **Cash Collections**: Sum of cash tender from sales created during the period that were not cancelled, plus cash repayments created during the period.
- **Cancellations Executed in Period**: Cancellations where `cancelled_at` falls within the period are displayed as deductions/adjustments.
- **Stock Restorations**: Inventory movements where `movement_type = 'sale_cancellation'` and `created_at` falls within the period.

### B. Effective Performance & Cohort Attribution (Transaction-Date Attribution)
*Focus: What were the true net sales and net debts generated by the business on that calendar date?*

- **Gross Sales on $T_1$**: `SUM(total_centavos)` for all sales created on $T_1$ (`created_at`).
- **Cancelled Sales on $T_1$**: `SUM(total_centavos)` for sales created on $T_1$ where `status = 'cancelled'`.
- **Net Sales on $T_1$**: `Gross Sales - Cancelled Sales`.

## 3. Worked Examples

### Scenario 1: Cash Sale Cancelled Later
- **Monday (Oct 10)**: Owner records cash sale `sale_1` for ₱200 (2 sachets of coffee). Cash collected = ₱200. Inventory reduced by 2.
- **Wednesday (Oct 12)**: Customer returns items; owner cancels `sale_1` with reason `"Isinauli ang kape"`.
  - `status` set to `'cancelled'`, `cancelled_at` set to Oct 12.
  - Inventory increased by 2 with movement `'sale_cancellation'`.
- **Monday Report**:
  - Gross Sales: ₱200.00
  - Cancelled Sales: ₱200.00
  - Net Sales: ₱0.00
- **Wednesday Activity Report**:
  - Displays 1 Cancellation Adjustment: `sale_1` (-₱200.00).
  - Stock Restorations: +2 coffee sachets.

### Scenario 2: Unpaid Credit Sale Cancelled
- **Tuesday (Oct 11)**: Mang Jose takes ₱150 goods on utang (`sale_2`). A `credit_entries` record `ce_2` is created with remaining balance ₱150.
- **Wednesday (Oct 12)**: Order was keyed in error; owner cancels `sale_2`.
  - `sale_2.status` becomes `'cancelled'`.
  - `ce_2.status` becomes `'cancelled'`, `ce_2.remaining_amount_centavos` becomes 0.
  - Mang Jose's total debt balance drops by ₱150 immediately.
  - Stock is restored once.

### Scenario 3: Paid-Down Credit Sale Guardrail
- **Monday (Oct 10)**: Aling Nena takes ₱300 goods on utang (`ce_3`).
- **Tuesday (Oct 11)**: Aling Nena pays ₱200 (`repay_1`, allocated to `ce_3`). Remaining debt on `ce_3` is now ₱100.
- **Wednesday (Oct 12)**: Attempting to cancel `sale_3` directly is **REJECTED** (`PaidCreditSaleCancellationError`) because ₱200 was already allocated against it.
  - **Resolution**: Owner must first explicitly reverse `repay_1` (reopening `ce_3` to ₱300).
  - Once repayments are reversed, `sale_3` can be cleanly cancelled, reducing Aling Nena's debt by ₱300 and restoring stock.

### Scenario 4: Cross-Debt Repayment Reversal
- **Oct 1**: Mang Teban has Debt A (₱100) and Debt B (₱100). Total debt = ₱200.
- **Oct 2**: Owner records repayment `repay_2` of ₱150.
  - Allocation 1: Debt A receives ₱100 (remaining: ₱0).
  - Allocation 2: Debt B receives ₱50 (remaining: ₱50).
  - Total debt = ₱50.
- **Oct 3**: Owner realizes repayment belonged to another customer and reverses `repay_2`:
  - `repay_2.status` becomes `'reversed'`.
  - Allocation 1 reversed: Debt A remaining becomes ₱0 + ₱100 = ₱100.
  - Allocation 2 reversed: Debt B remaining becomes ₱50 + ₱50 = ₱100.
  - Mang Teban's total debt is restored to ₱200 atomically.
  - Preserves original debt age and timestamps.

## 4. Canonical SQL Patterns for Report Queries (Ticket #15 Coordination)

### 4.1 Net Sales in Date Range
```sql
SELECT 
  COALESCE(SUM(CASE WHEN status != 'cancelled' THEN total_centavos ELSE 0 END), 0) AS net_sales_centavos,
  COALESCE(SUM(CASE WHEN status = 'cancelled' THEN total_centavos ELSE 0 END), 0) AS cancelled_sales_centavos,
  COALESCE(SUM(total_centavos), 0) AS gross_sales_centavos
FROM sales
WHERE created_at >= ? AND created_at <= ?;
```

### 4.2 Cash Collections in Date Range
```sql
SELECT 
  -- Cash from active cash sales
  (SELECT COALESCE(SUM(total_centavos), 0) 
   FROM sales 
   WHERE payment_method = 'cash' 
     AND status != 'cancelled' 
     AND created_at >= ? AND created_at <= ?)
  +
  -- Cash from active repayments
  (SELECT COALESCE(SUM(amount_centavos), 0) 
   FROM credit_repayments 
   WHERE payment_method = 'cash' 
     AND status != 'reversed' 
     AND created_at >= ? AND created_at <= ?)
  AS total_cash_collections_centavos;
```
